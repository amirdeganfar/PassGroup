What sits underneath the screens

Think of a restaurant. Diners see the dining room, but most of the work happens in the kitchen, the storeroom and the books. An app is the same. The screens are the dining room, and everything else is out the back.

Why does app development take so long? Testing and review

An app that works on your phone can still fail on someone else's. So studios test on a spread of real devices, then fix what breaks. Both app stores then review the app. Apple says it typically reviews at least half of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours. A rejection means a fix and a resubmit, so we plan some buffer. Our page on app testing costs explains what good testing includes.

What makes it take even longer

Most delays come from the plan changing, not from slow coding. These are the usual causes:

  • Features added mid-build. Each one needs design, building and testing.
  • Slow approvals. A design waiting a week for sign-off is a week lost.
  • Unclear scope. If nobody wrote down what "done" means, the project keeps growing.
  • Outside systems. Linking to another company's software often hides surprises.

How to get to launch sooner

The reliable way is a smaller first version. Launch the core features, then add more once real users show you what they want. Our guide to how long an MVP takes covers this in detail. An MVP is just the simplest version of your app that people can really use.

Clear briefs and quick feedback help too. One of the best signs in a project is a client who answers questions within a day. That alone can save weeks.
