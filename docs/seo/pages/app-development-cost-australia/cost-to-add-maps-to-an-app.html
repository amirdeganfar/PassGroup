The map itself is the cheap part — that surprises most founders. How much does it cost to add maps to an app? A basic map — showing a pin, a suburb, your store locations — is a modest build on top of a working app. Live tracking, routes and geofencing cost more, and monthly mapping fees grow with how much your users use it. In the wider budget, the app development cost guide for Australia shows where features like this fit.

A basic map (modest build)

  • Show one or more locations on a map
  • Tap a pin for details or a directions link
  • Small build on an app that already works

Advanced mapping (costs more)

  • Live tracking — watch a driver move in real time
  • Routes and travel times computed in the app
  • Geofencing — the app reacts when a phone enters an area

How much does it cost to add maps to an app for live tracking?

Meaningfully more. Moving markers mean constant updates, which drains phone batteries and needs location permissions handled carefully — plus extra build and test time. It's also where the monthly fees climb, because every position update is a billable call. Delivery apps run on exactly this: what a food delivery app costs shows that end of the range.

The monthly fee nobody budgets for

Mapping services charge per use, and the fees grow with your user count. Google's mapping platform, for example, starts at $100 a month for 50,000 uses on its entry plan — its pricing page has the current rates. Lighter apps can pay less paying per use, but the pattern holds: an app's monthly running bill always has a map line in it once you add maps.

$100/mothe entry plan on Google's mapping platform for 50,000 uses — small apps can pay less, busy apps pay more

I've watched this bite a client once: the app took off, and the first big map bill arrived before the revenue did. The fix costs nothing.

Map fees scale with usage, not with your revenue. Ask your developer for billing alerts and spending caps before launch — not after the first surprise invoice.

Short version: a modest build for a basic map, more for tracking and routes, and a monthly fee that grows with usage. Cap it with alerts and it never surprises you.
