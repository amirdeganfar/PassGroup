How much does it cost to make an iOS app: what the budget covers

An iOS app is written in Swift, Apple's own programming language, for one family of devices. A lean budget typically includes:

  • Design and build for iPhone screens, with one core job done well
  • Testing on a small set of recent iPhones
  • App Store submission and the review back-and-forth
  • Apple's account fee, US$99 a year, paid by you
  • Not included: an Android version, ongoing updates, marketing

Features move the number most. Logins, payments and maps each add real hours, which is why a simple app costs a fraction of one with a back office.

Why iOS-only can be the cheaper first step

Apple makes fewer phone models than the Android world, so there are fewer screen sizes to test. That keeps the testing line small, and you learn one store and one set of rules.

The trade-off is reach. In Australia, iPhones are popular, but you'd still miss everyone on Android. If your first users are likely to carry iPhones, starting there is a sound way to keep an MVP (the smallest working version of your app) affordable.

The iOS costs people forget

Apple's Developer Program fee is US$99 per year, and it renews every year. If you let it lapse, your app can be pulled from the store. Open the account in your own business name, not your developer's.

Apple reviews every new app and every update before it goes live. Reviews usually take days, but a rejection means fixes and a resubmit, so leave room before any launch date. For commissions, see our app store fees guide, and for after launch, what updates cost.

Is it cheaper to make an iOS app than an Android app?

Usually the build costs are close. iOS tends to need slightly less testing, but Apple's fee is yearly while Google's is one-off. The bigger saving comes from choosing one platform instead of two.
