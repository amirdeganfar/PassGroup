What you're actually paying for

Two things, and they behave differently. First, a one-off: your developer connects the app to the AI service through its API — the plug that lets two programs talk — and builds the screens around it. Second, a meter that never stops: the service charges per use, so the bill grows with your users. It's the same logic as the rest of what it costs to run an app, with a faster dial.

Our breakdown of what apps cost to make in Australia puts a serious AI feature at $15,000 or more on its own, with full AI-heavy platforms in the six-figure bands. One warning: adding AI features is a different project from building an app using AI tools — different question, different budget.

The bill that bites isn't the build — it's month nine, when the AI feature gets popular. Price the usage before you approve the feature.

Start with a paid service, not a custom model

A custom model is AI trained on your own data. It's the version people picture when they say "build us AI" — and it costs much more to create, and needs looking after forever. The Australian government's AI guidance for business puts it plainly: off-the-shelf AI tools are cheaper, quicker and more reliable; custom solutions cost more and need upkeep. For version one, the paid service wins.

Do

  • Rent a proven AI service
  • Price the monthly usage before the build
  • Start with one feature users actually need

Don't

  • Train a custom model for version one
  • Assume the AI bill is flat each month
  • Add AI just for the pitch deck

So, how much does it cost to add AI to an app, really?

Modest to build, metered to run — and the run part is the one to pressure-test. Every second brief asks for "AI, like ChatGPT". My first question back: how many questions a day will users ask?
