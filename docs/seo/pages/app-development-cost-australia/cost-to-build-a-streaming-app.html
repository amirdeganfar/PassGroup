How much does it cost to build a streaming app for live video?

Live is the expensive version. Instead of playing stored files, your app has to move video as it happens, which means stronger servers, a chat feature that keeps up, and something called transcoding — converting each stream into the sizes and speeds every phone and internet speed needs. Budget comfortably above the on-demand price for that. A slower, cheaper build is possible, but live video rarely is.

Even stored video keeps costing you after launch. Phones can't all play the same file, so apps use a standard that chops movies into small chunks and adjusts quality to each viewer's internet — Apple's HTTP Live Streaming guide explains the technology. Storing those files and delivering them is what your monthly app hosting cost covers, and video makes that bill climb faster than almost any other app type.

The pattern I see most: founders test streaming costs with ten users, then get surprised by the bill at a thousand. Video delivery doesn't grow politely. Price for the audience you want, not the one you have today.

Starting narrow keeps the build honest. You don't need every feature a big platform has on day one — a focused video app for one audience is a real product, and it's how most sensible launches happen. Our cost breakdown for an Instagram-style app shows the same idea: more media features, more money, so cut what you can before you build.

Can I build a streaming app cheaper than that?

Sometimes, by trimming scope: one video type, no live mode, fewer screens. The build gets cheaper, but hosting stays tied to how many people watch and for how long — that part never really goes away.

What does streaming hosting cost each month?

It's usage-based, so it moves with your audience and watch time. There's no flat figure that holds for everyone — ask your developer to model it on real viewing numbers before you commit.
