What the US$99 covers (and what it doesn't)

The Apple Developer Program membership is a rental, not a purchase. Pay US$99 and you can publish apps, run beta tests through TestFlight (Apple's test-the-app service) and use Apple's tools while the membership stays current; stop paying and your apps come off the store. A free Apple developer account exists, but it can't publish to the App Store — it's for building and testing on your own devices. The US$299 enterprise tier is for private staff apps — ignore it.

Listing, hosting and reviews are free — the fee people feel is commission. Sell digital goods inside the app and Apple keeps 15% for most small developers under its Small Business Program (30% at the standard rate). Where founders get caught isn't the fee, it's the discipline: the account renews on Apple's schedule (Google Play's US$25 registration, by contrast, never renews), and a lapsed membership can pull a live app offline. One founder we know forgot the renewal entirely — the app went dark eleven months in, and restoring it cost more than a decade of fees.

Budget the account as a recurring yearly line item — US$99 plus a little exchange-rate drift — never as a one-off setup cost.

So how much does it cost to put an app on the App Store in total?

US$99 a year for the account, plus the build, plus 15–30% of digital sales. Listing, hosting and reviews are free.

Is the fee the same in Australia?

The fee is set in US dollars and billed in local currency, so the AUD amount moves with exchange rates — check at renewal.
