What app development grants actually exist right now

The app development grants Australia actually funds in 2026 fall into three broad groups. First, tax offsets: the R&D tax incentive isn't a cheque, but it returns a share of eligible research and development spending at tax time — the current rules and registration sit on the official R&D Tax Incentive portal. Second, matched grants for defined stages, such as getting a novel product ready to sell or pushing into export markets. Third, state programs, which change with policy and are best found through the one official finder rather than remembered from old blog posts.

Where the money comes from: national vs state programs

National programs lean towards research and commercialisation: turning a novel product into something sellable, here or overseas. State programs are broader and shift with each budget — grants for hiring, for regional businesses, for exporters, for young companies in favoured industries. Because every state runs its own mix, the useful habit is filtering the finder by your state and industry, then checking "coming soon" as well as "open". Upcoming rounds are worth a diary note; they close fast and some are first-in, first-served.

It's also worth knowing what the state money usually looks like. Some states fund innovation directly: co-funded grants for developing a new product, or vouchers that pay part of a project with a local studio. Others fund the runway instead — programs for hiring, training or export readiness — which frees your own cash for the build. Reading the program's objective tells you which kind you're looking at before you spend a weekend on an application.

One more national door: GrantConnect, the government's official register of announced grant opportunities. It's clunky but authoritative — if a program is real, it's listed there.

What grants typically pay for (and never pay for)

Grants usually help with

  • A defined stage: prototype, pilot, first sales, export push
  • Eligible R&D activity, with records kept from day one
  • Projects that create jobs or grow the local industry

Grants never cover

  • Your whole build invoice, end to end
  • Everyday running costs like hosting and subscriptions
  • Money you already spent before approval

Read the right-hand column twice. It's where most first-timers get burned: grants pay part of a defined stage, not the whole project, and almost never retrospectively.

No grant covers 100% of an app build. Treat every program as a co-investor that pays a slice of one stage — and budget as if the grant arrives late or not at all.

Who qualifies: the usual requirements

Details vary by program, but the checklist overlaps. You'll generally need an Australian Business Number (ABN) and a registered structure, often a company. There's an aggregated turnover cap that counts connected entities, not just yours. The product or process must be genuinely novel — software that does something new, not a familiar app with a fresh logo. You'll need co-funding of your own, because matched means matched. And you'll need records to prove every claim. From what we see, founders with even one paying pilot customer write the strongest novelty sections — the evidence does the arguing.

Two quiet disqualifiers are worth naming. Spending before approval rarely counts — most programs assess the project from a start date they set. And grants aimed at "growth" usually want outcomes they can measure: jobs created, revenue added, markets entered. If your project can't be described in those terms, either reframe it honestly or wait for a better-fit round.

The application, in plain steps

  1. Search the finder

    Filter by state, industry and stage. Shortlist anything open that fits your project, not your wish list.

  2. Read the eligibility twice

    Turnover caps, structure, project stage. If one criterion misses, move on — don't fund a rewrite of your business to chase it.

  3. Call the program contact

    Most list one. Ten minutes on the phone tells you more than a week of guessing.

  4. Write to the criteria

    Novelty, jobs, dollars matched, dates. Mirror their words back at them, in that order.

  5. Plan for the wait

    Assessments take months and pay on milestones. Build your timeline around the gap, not the announcement.

Grants vs R&D incentive vs plain budgeting

Grants are milestone money for defined stages: competitive, slow, and never guaranteed. The R&D tax incentive is repeatable — if your build involves genuine experimentation, a share of the spend comes back at tax time, and the portal linked above holds the current rates and registration steps. Plain budgeting is the floor under both: the leanest plan is a smallest-sellable MVP paid for in stages, sized against a realistic app budget that includes running costs and marketing. The founders who end up with grants treat them as an accelerator on a plan that already worked — never as the plan itself.

One caveat before the final word: this page is general information, not tax or financial advice. Grant rules and tax treatments change — confirm your position with your registered tax agent before you commit money.
