US$25one-off Google Play registration — no annual renewal

Google's Play Console help sets out the registration fee and payment process. Pay it, verify who you are, and it's yours for good — the opposite of Apple's yearly rental, and one quiet reason Android feels cheaper to keep alive. Both stores, side by side, in our app store fees breakdown.

What you get for US$25

The account, for life: unlimited listings, the Play Console dashboard and the whole Android storefront. But two steps trip new founders, and neither costs money:

Before your app can go live

  • Closed testing first. New personal accounts must run a closed test — a private release to real testers — before Google grants production access. The rules live in Play Console and have tightened over the years — check before you promise a launch date.
  • Identity verification. Google checks who you are before your listing goes public. Free, but expect a wait — build it into your timeline.

Where the rest of the budget goes

Sell digital goods — subscriptions, unlocks, credits — and Google keeps 15% of the first US$1 million you earn each year, 30% beyond that. Sell nothing in-app and it never starts. The store side, on one screen:

A founder we worked with scheduled launch around the build and forgot the testing window — the closed test and verification added a fortnight nobody had budgeted. The US$25 never changed their plan; the process around it did. The fee is still the smallest line: what Android app development costs dwarfs everything on this page.

So how much does it cost to put an app on Google Play in total?

US$25 once for the account. Listing, hosting and updates are free; commission (15–30%) applies only to digital sales.

Is there a yearly fee like Apple's?

No — Google's registration is a one-off. Apple charges US$99 every year for the equivalent account.

Google's side of the ledger is small and mostly paid once — plan the process, not the fee.
