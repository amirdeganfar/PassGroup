First, wages: Melbourne developers and designers command Sydney-level salaries, and studio rates track that. Second, expectations: founders here usually arrive wanting the polished thing, not the duct-taped one — which is the right instinct, but it costs.

Melbourne rates vs the national picture

Australia is a premium market globally, and within it, Melbourne sits at the top of the band with Sydney — the same $120–$200 hourly range for agencies that the national guides quote. What differs is the mix beneath that average. Melbourne's market has a deep bench of small and mid-sized studios concentrated in the CBD and inner suburbs — Collingwood, Fitzroy, North Melbourne — alongside the big names, which means a founder budgeting $30,000–$80,000 has more genuine options here than the headline rates suggest. The city also feeds itself: a dense cluster of universities turning out design and engineering graduates, and a startup scene that keeps senior people in town. For a buyer, that competition is good news — quotes vary, and the second and third quotes are routinely better than the first.

What a founder-budget build costs here

With $20,000–$50,000 — the honest founder budget for a first app — a good Melbourne studio can deliver a focused build: one core job done properly, professional design, tested on real phones, submitted to both stores. As a rough shape for a mid-band $35,000 project, expect design to take about $7,000–$9,000, development the largest share at $20,000–$23,000, and testing, store submission and polish the rest. Those proportions hold across most first builds, whatever the total. What that budget does not buy is every idea at once, and the studios worth hiring will say so. The smartest version of this budget funds the first thing users actually do, then lets usage data — not enthusiasm — pick what gets built second. Budget for year two before you sign year one. Apps need updates every year — new phone software alone guarantees it — so whatever you spend building, hold back roughly a fifth of it for the twelve months after launch.

What Melbourne agencies include in quotes

A quote from an established Melbourne studio typically bundles more than code: discovery workshops, UX and visual design, testing on real devices, store submission, and a warranty window after launch. One of the city's most established app studios publishes price guidance of roughly $50,000 to $250,000+ for production builds, with most ready-for-market MVPs between $125,000 and $250,000 — consistent with what we see on the jobs that come to us. When you compare quotes, check which of those inclusions are inside the number and which get billed later; our breakdown of what an agency quote actually covers gives you the line items to look for. It's also fair to ask where the work happens: a genuine Melbourne team means the people designing your app can meet you in a room — which matters more over a ten-month build than a 5% rate difference.

Sydney vs Melbourne: does the city change the price?

Short answer: less than founders expect. Both cities draw from the same national talent pool and charge at the top of the Australian band, and for most projects the rates land within noise of each other. The differences are structural, not dramatic: Sydney runs heavier on corporate and fintech work and carries Sydney salaries; Melbourne has proportionally more small studios and design-led shops, which gives budget-conscious founders more choice in the middle of the market. With Greater Melbourne's population now above five million — the ABS keeps the running count — the local market supports both scenes comfortably. For most Melbourne founders, the right question isn't "which city" but "which team" — and there are excellent and terrible teams in both.

Questions to ask a Melbourne studio

Before you shortlist, take these to the first meeting:

  • Who exactly builds it? Names, roles, and how much of the project the seniors stay on
  • What's inside the quote? Design, testing, store submission, warranty — or billed later?
  • How do payments run? Milestones tied to demonstrated work, never everything up front
  • What do I own at the end? Code, accounts and IP assigned to you, in writing
  • What happens in month twelve? Update costs, maintenance terms, and who answers the phone
  • Can I talk to a past client? One whose app is live and similar in size to yours

A studio that answers all six plainly is worth its rate. One that fudges the ownership or maintenance questions is telling you something — politely.
