The headline: offshore rates in AUD

Rates vary by country, seniority and who you're hiring through. As rough bands from live market rates, expect something like this:

Eastern EuropeA$60–$110Strong engineering; still a timezone gap from AU
Australian freelancer$80–$150One person's capacity, skills and availability
Australian agency$120–$200Team depth, but the highest rate on the page

Two small print items sit inside those bands. First, marketplace markups: hire through a platform rather than a direct contractor, and the platform typically takes a cut on top — the developer keeps less than you pay. Second, seniority: every country's band has juniors at the bottom and leads at the top, and a bargain rate usually means the bargain end. Ask what experience level the quote assumes, and who reviews their work.

Those offshore bands are why the pitch writes itself: same app, a third of the rate. The rest of this page is about why the pitch usually survives contact with reality only partially.

Why the hourly rate isn't the final bill

A rate is a unit price. Your bill is the unit price times the actual number of hours — and that second number is where offshore quotes go quiet. To win the job, quotes can assume perfect requirements, zero rework, and a scope that never moves. When any of those assumptions breaks, the "extra" hours come back to you as change requests.

The rate also buys different things in different places. An Australian agency rate includes project management, design, testing and someone senior reviewing the work. A bare offshore rate buys code and little else — those other jobs don't vanish, they just move to someone else's invoice. Usually yours. Our page on the costs nobody quotes you covers how this plays out in both local and offshore builds.

Here's the rate-maths in one worked example — illustrative numbers, not a quote. Say your app needs roughly 300 build hours. At A$40 offshore that's A$12,000; at A$140 local it's A$42,000. Now add what the offshore quote left out: design bought separately, testing squeezed into your launch week, two weeks of rework on a misunderstood flow, and a couple of your own evenings spent project managing. The gap doesn't close to zero — offshore can still win on paper — but it's rarely the two-thirds saving the rate card promised, and it's worth knowing that before you sign rather than after.

The hidden costs: rework, delays, management time

Three costs show up on almost every offshore project that underpriced the work:

  • Rework — a requirement that took one sentence to write and three weeks to misunderstand. Building it twice doubles that line of the budget.
  • Delays — every timezone-separated question costs a day, and a day lost mid-sprint pushes everything behind it.
  • Management time — with a local team, coordination is someone else's overhead. Offshore, you're the project manager, and those hours are unpaid and unbudgeted.

None of these appear on the quote. All of them appear on the invoice.

The most expensive sentence in offshore development is "they should have assumed that". If a requirement isn't written down, tested with a prototype, or demonstrated in a call, don't assume it — price it in or cut it.

Communication and timezone realities

Melbourne is two to three hours behind most of South-East Asia and up to ten behind Eastern Europe. A two-hour overlap is workable for a team that's done it before. No overlap means every blocked question waits overnight, and a project with daily blockers drifts for weeks.

You can buy the overlap back with process, and it's worth doing before day one. Set fixed overlap hours for calls, and protect them. Put every decision in writing the same day — a shared document beats a good memory across ten timezones. And ask for weekly demos of working software, not weekly status reports. A demo can't hide a slipped week the way a status report can.

From experience

A founder came to us after an offshore build that quoted about 40% below every local bid — and on paper, the rate maths held up. What the quote didn't hold was scope: every "small misunderstanding" was invoiced as a change request, and by month three the saving was gone. The app now lives in a repository we can't name and a timezone we schedule our Tuesdays around.

None of that was bad luck. It was a rate quote where a project plan should have been.

When offshore genuinely works

Offshore isn't a scam — it's a trade, and some projects suit it far better than others:

Offshore suits builds that

  • Have a written, tested spec and a settled scope
  • Have someone on your side managing the project daily
  • Use a vetted team you've trialled on paid sample work
  • Are extensions or add-ons to an existing system, not brand-new products

Think twice when

  • The idea is still shaping up as you go
  • Nobody on your side can review code or designs
  • The quote skips discovery, design and testing lines
  • Launch date is fixed and the market will notice a slip

Comparing offshore app development cost with a local quote

Put the two quotes side by side and force them onto the same page — literally. Check each one against four questions. Does it price the whole job (discovery, design, testing, launch), or just the coding? Who pays when the scope changes? Who holds the intellectual property — under Australian defaults, contractors often start with rights you assumed you were buying, and cross-border contracts make it messier. IP Australia's plain-English IP guide is worth ten minutes before you sign anything. And what happens after launch, when the app needs its yearly updates? A fair comparison adds those four answers to both sides. Only then do the numbers mean anything — and once they do, the offshore app development cost is often closer to local than the rate card promised.
