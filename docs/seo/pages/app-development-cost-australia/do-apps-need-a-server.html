Do apps need a server? Almost every useful one does. A server — a computer your app's data lives on, usually rented in a data centre — stores accounts, content and payments even when the app itself looks local. A calculator that works fully offline is a rare exception. Server costs are part of your running bill from day one.

Works fully offline (rare)

  • Everything happens on the phone itself
  • No accounts, no shared content, no payments
  • Think calculator, compass, simple notes

Needs a server (most apps)

  • Saves logins, photos, messages, orders
  • Syncs data between users and devices
  • Follows a user when they change phones

What the server actually does all day

The app on the phone is the shopfront; the server is the stockroom. When someone creates an account, uploads a photo or pays, that information has to live somewhere safe and always-on. That's why even a "simple" app is really two products — the screens people see, and the machinery behind them. The app development cost guide for Australia shows how that machinery shapes both the build and the running budget. And if accounts are the feature dragging you into all this, our guide to adding user logins shows what that one piece costs.

None of this means racks in your office. Most small apps rent hosting from big cloud providers — think of it as renting a small, very reliable locker online — and the developer sets it up as part of the build. What you're really paying for is three jobs done continuously: keeping data safe, keeping the app reachable, and scaling up as more people arrive. Each job is invisible on a good day; you only meet them when they fail.

The tell I look for in founder demos: everything works because it's on one phone. Ask where the account list lives when a second user signs in from another device — if the answer is "nowhere yet", you need a server.

Where server costs land on your bill

Hosting is usually a small monthly figure at first, but it grows with users and data — and it never turns off. Do apps need a server even when users pay nothing? Yes; free apps carry these bills too. Two more things ride on the hosting. Backups and security keep user records safe, and if your app stores personal information, Australian law expects you to protect it — the OAIC's notifiable data breaches scheme explains when a leak must be reported.
