The app doesn't die the day the invoice stops — what happens if I stop paying for app maintenance is slower and quieter than that. The app keeps working at first, but nothing gets fixed: phone updates, bugs and expired certificates slowly break it, often within a year. A hosting lapse can take it offline much sooner.

Every one of those breakages is preventable, and the running cost is a fraction of the build — our app development cost guide for Australia puts maintenance in context next to what you paid to build. Here's how the decay actually unfolds.

  • Months 0–3: quiet. Nothing has broken yet — which is exactly why this trap works.
  • Around month 6: a phone update or a small bug needs a fix. Nobody ships one.
  • Within a year: signing certificates — the digital stamps that prove your updates are genuine — expire or stop matching the new phone systems, and the app can no longer be updated.

The loud break: stores and servers

Two failure points matter most. The first is the app stores: every update must be signed with a current developer account and re-approved, so a lapsed account or an unpatched bug can bench the app at its next review. The second is hosting: if that bill lapses, the server can go dark and take user logins and data with it. Maintenance is really insurance for the build you already paid for — the same way insurance protects what you already own.

Never let hosting and backups lapse while users still have accounts. A dark server means lost data and lost trust — and neither comes back cheaply.

Fixing an app that fell behind

The good news: little is usually lost forever. An app that slid for a year typically needs a catch-up round of fixes, certificate renewals and testing before anything new gets built — weeks of work, not a rebuild. The exception is an app that sat through a major operating-system change; those drift closer to starting over, and it's worth weighing that against what apps cost to build in Australia before deciding. In our studio, the rescue calls follow the same script every time: nobody noticed for months, then one update broke everything at once.

What happens if I stop paying for app maintenance but keep the hosting on?

It stays online but still decays — phone updates and expiring certificates don't care that you're paying. Expect a catch-up bill when you resume; typical running figures are in the cost guide for Australia.
