Can I claim app development on tax? Usually yes. Most costs a business pays to build or run its own app are claimable — some come straight off in the year you pay them, others count as an asset and are claimed over a few years. Which bucket applies depends on your structure, your GST registration and the timing, so treat this as the map and let a registered tax agent navigate. Two buckets decide it: running costs are claimed in the year you pay them; the build itself is often an asset, claimed over time — unless it fits a small-business instant write-off.

What usually comes off in the same year

Hosting, monitoring, store renewals and software you're billed for periodically — all deducted in the year you pay. Smaller builds can go the same way: for small businesses using the simplified depreciation rules, an asset under the current instant asset write-off limit can be deducted in full once it's ready for use. Software has its own set of paths, laid out in the ATO's in-house software rules — and a build with genuine experimentation may also qualify for the R&D tax incentive.

When the build is treated as an asset

A custom app over the threshold is "in-house software" to the ATO: over-limit builds go to a small business pool, costs while the app is still in development can sit in a software development pool, and the rest is depreciated over its effective life. From experience, the detail agents ask about first is the year the app was first ready for use — that usually decides which year the big claim lands in.

This is general information, not tax or financial advice — structure, GST and timing change the answer. Confirm with your registered tax agent before you lodge.

What records do I need to claim app development costs?

Contracts, tax invoices showing the developer's ABN and GST, payment records, and milestone approvals — enough to show what was built, when it was paid for, and when it went live.
