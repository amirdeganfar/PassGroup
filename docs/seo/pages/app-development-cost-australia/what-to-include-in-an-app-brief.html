A good one-page brief covers six things: the problem you're solving, who will use the app, your five or so must-have features, how people will pay (if they do), your budget range and a deadline. If you're wondering what should I include in an app brief, that's the list. A clear brief gets you far more accurate quotes, because developers stop guessing.

A brief is simply a short document you send to developers so they can quote on the same thing. It doesn't need to be technical. Our app development cost guide shows what typical builds cost, which helps you set the budget line.

What should I include in an app brief? The six parts

  1. The problem

    One or two sentences. For example, "Our clients book by phone and we lose bookings after hours."

  2. The users

    Who opens this app? Customers, staff, or both? A line on each is enough.

  3. Must-have features

    List about five. Mark anything else as "later". This is where budgets get decided.

  4. How it earns or saves money

    Subscriptions, one-off sales, bookings or internal savings. If it needs payments, say so.

  5. Budget range

    A range, such as "$20,000 to $40,000". Developers can then suggest what fits instead of guessing.

  6. Deadline and why

    A launch date plus the reason, like a trade show or busy season. The reason helps them plan.

Business.gov.au advises hirers to have a clear idea of what they want before negotiating a contract. The same goes for a brief. It's your first chance to say what you want, so say it in writing.

Extras that make your brief stronger

Add two or three apps you like and one line on why. "We like how Uber shows a live map" tells a developer more than a page of description.

Also mention anything the app must connect to, such as a booking system, accounting software or an existing website. Say if you already have branding or designs. And say what you've already tried, like a no-code prototype, because that saves time.

You can leave out the tech details. If you don't know whether you need iOS, Android or both, say so and ask for options. Phasing the build, which means launching the core first and adding extras later, is often smart. Our page on phased app development explains how.
