Why Android testing adds to the cost of making an Android app

iPhones come from one maker. Android phones come from dozens, with different screen sizes, speeds and software versions. An app that looks perfect on one can break on another. So studios test on a spread of real devices, not just one or two.

So how much does it cost to make an Android app compared with iPhone? That extra checking is where Android can edge above iOS in the budget. It isn't a huge gap for a simple app, but it grows with complexity. Our page on app testing costs explains what's normally included.

Google Play's account rules and fee

Google charges a one-off fee to publish. The Play Console registration fee is US$25, paid once, with no yearly renewal. That's cheaper over time than Apple's yearly fee.

Google sets extra testing requirements for newer personal developer accounts before an app can go live. Check the current rules on Google's page, and ask your studio whether a business account suits you better.

There's also a commission if you sell digital goods in the app. We cover how both stores charge in app store fees explained.

Android-only or both platforms?

Android-only can suit apps for a specific audience, such as field staff using company phones. For a public app, most founders want both stores. Building both together, ideally as one cross-platform app, usually costs less than building them one after the other.

The reason is shared work. Design, planning and the server behind the app get done once. Building the second platform later means paying to re-test and re-check things that could have been done together, which is why we usually suggest building in phases only when budget forces it.

Is an Android app cheaper than an iPhone app?

The build price is typically similar. Android can need more device testing, but Google's one-off US$25 fee is lighter than Apple's yearly US$99.
