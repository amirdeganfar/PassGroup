How much does it cost to make a banking app versus adding payments?

A genuine banking app means holding customers' money, and that means regulation. Australia's banking regulator, APRA, sets the standards a licensed bank must meet — and building to those standards is most of the cost. Think independent security audits, fraud systems that watch every transaction, and compliance reviews that run the whole project. A payments or wallet feature, on the other hand, lets users pay inside a normal app while licensed payment providers carry most of that regulatory weight for you.

A real banking app

  • Holds customers' money and deposits
  • Needs its own licensing and regulator sign-off
  • Six figures and up, then audits forever

A payments feature in your app

  • Runs through licensed payment providers
  • You never touch or hold the money
  • A fraction of the cost, in weeks to months

Most founders I talk to about this actually want the right-hand column. They want people to pay inside their app, not to become a bank. Naming that honestly at the start saves a fortune — and it keeps you out of a regulated industry you never needed to enter.

Which brings me to the warning I give every founder: if a quote promises bank-grade security for a bargain price, walk away. Real security work is slow and expensive, and that's exactly why it shows up in honest quotes.
