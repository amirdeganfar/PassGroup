The costs hiding behind "free"

Three bills survive every DIY build. Developer accounts: both stores charge to publish — Apple about US$99 a year (roughly AU$149), Google a one-off US$25. Hosting: the computers that keep your app running, typically $50–$500 a month once real people use it. Maintenance: every phone update can break something, so fixes never really stop.

Small bills next to agency fees — but they still deserve a line in a budget. Moneysmart's free budgeting guides are a painless way to give them one.

Prototype free, then spend. A $0 clickable demo answers most questions an investor or developer will ask — build that first, pay for code only once people have tried it.

When free tools are the smart move

Free is the right price in three situations: testing an idea before you commit, showing investors something clickable instead of a slide, or running a simple internal tool. In all three, if the idea flops you've lost weekends, not savings.

Where free stops working: anything that stores other people's data, takes payments or needs to grow. Those needs pull you back into the paid bands fast.

So, can you build an app for free?

A demo, yes. A published product people rely on, no — and that's fine. Spend nothing to learn what to build, budget for the compulsory costs, treat anything bigger as a proper project.
