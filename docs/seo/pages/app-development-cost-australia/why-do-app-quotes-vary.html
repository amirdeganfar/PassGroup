Quotes vary because each developer is pricing something different: the scope, the quality, who does the work and what's left out. A $10,000 and a $60,000 quote for "the same app" usually differ in features, design depth, testing and support. So when you ask why do app development quotes vary so much, the answer is that you're rarely comparing like with like. Compare scope first and price second.

A quote is a list of assumptions with a price on the end. Two different lists will never give the same total.

Our guide to app development costs in Australia shows the typical bands, and the gap between them is wide for good reason. Here are the four things that move a quote most.

Why do app development quotes vary so much? Four causes

  • Scope. Ten screens cost less than forty. One developer may include an admin dashboard and payments while another assumes you'll add them later.
  • Design depth. Custom design takes real time. A tidy template is cheaper and looks like one.
  • Who does the work. A solo freelancer, an offshore team and a local studio carry very different costs. See our look at freelance versus agency app development for how those differ.
  • Testing and support. Careful testing on real phones and a bug-fix period after launch take time. Cheap quotes often leave both out.

Think of it like quotes for a kitchen renovation. One includes plumbing, tiles and a plasterer. Another covers cabinets only. Neither is lying, but the totals can't be compared until you line up what each one covers.

How to compare two quotes fairly

Cheaper quote, check for

  • Missing testing or launch help
  • Template design
  • No bug-fix period
  • Vague wording on features

Pricier quote, check for

  • Features you don't need yet
  • Senior hours on simple tasks
  • Extras bundled in by default
  • Costs that could be phased

Put the quotes side by side and match them block by block: planning, design, build, testing and launch. Whatever has no match on the other quote is your difference (three is usually the right number of quotes to collect). Ask each developer to list what's excluded. Business.gov.au notes that a good contract should state what each party will do, and a good quote should do the same.
