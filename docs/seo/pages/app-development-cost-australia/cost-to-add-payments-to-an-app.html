"Can't we just add a pay button?" — that's usually how this conversation starts, and it's the right instinct: taking money inside the app removes friction. How much does it cost to add payments to an app? Usually a few thousand dollars on an app that already works — more for subscriptions or marketplaces, plus small fees on every sale from then on. To see where this sits in the big picture, the app development cost guide for Australia covers the whole budget.

Budget a few thousand dollars to add standard card payments to a working app — then a small fee on every sale, forever.

What the money buys once

A payment gateway — the service that actually processes cards, like Stripe or PayPal — is quick to wire in. What costs the time is the work around it: security checks so card data is handled the way the PCI DSS standard requires (the global card-industry security rules), and extra testing, because you can't ship a payment button that sometimes fails. Payment paths are a big reason app testing costs what it does.

The fees that come after

Two cuts ride on every sale. The gateway takes a small percentage plus a few cents per transaction — providers vary, so compare. And for digital goods bought inside the app (unlocks, credits, subscriptions), Apple and Google take their own slice — store fees that typically run 15–30%. Selling real goods or real-world services skips that store cut. These are the classic hidden costs that follow the build — small on day one, real by year's end.

How much does it cost to add payments to an app with subscriptions?

More — this is where quotes climb. Recurring billing adds real work: retrying failed cards, upgrades, cancellations. Marketplaces cost more again, because money must split between sellers and refunds and disputes need handling. Both are bigger jobs — weeks, not days.

The surprise in client budgets is never the build — it's the fee on every sale. Founders shop hard on the build price, then shrug at the per-transaction rate. Compare both, always.

Short version: a few thousand dollars for standard card payments on a working app, a small percentage on every sale, and a bigger bill only when subscriptions or marketplaces enter the picture.
