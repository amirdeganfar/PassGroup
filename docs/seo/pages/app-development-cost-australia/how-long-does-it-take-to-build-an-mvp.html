How long does it take to build an MVP? Usually 6–12 weeks for a focused one: a few weeks of design and planning, then 4–8 weeks of building and testing. Bigger feature lists stretch the build toward six months. An MVP — minimum viable product — is just the simplest version of your app that real people can use.

Time and money move together — we follow the money side in how apps get priced in Australia. This page sticks to the calendar, and to the promises worth checking before you sign anything.

We run these builds every month at Pass Group, and the same force sets the timeline every time: the size of the feature list, not the speed of the developers.

Scope is the clock. Every feature you add to version one adds design, build and testing time — cutting scope is the only reliable way to cut weeks.

Stages overlap a little, so the calendar total is usually 6–12 weeks rather than the sum of the rows. The government's guide to developing a new product on business.gov.au walks the same path for physical goods.

Why two-week promises rarely survive

A two-week "MVP" is usually a clickable mockup, not a product — no real users, no payments, no store listing. Ask what the quote leaves out before you celebrate the price.

Three things eat the shortcut. Real features carry edge cases nobody predicted. Apple reviews every app before it goes live, which can take days. Testing with real users always surfaces changes — the entire point of an MVP — so build time for it.

A café founder we worked with insisted loyalty points belonged in version one. We talked him into holding them for version two, launched in nine weeks, and the points system later cost half what it would have — by then we knew what customers wanted.
