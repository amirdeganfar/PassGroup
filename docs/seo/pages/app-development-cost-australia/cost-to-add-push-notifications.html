Short answer: it's one of the cheapest features you can add. A push notification is the alert that pops up on a phone when your app wants attention, even while it's closed. So how much does it cost to add push notifications to an app? For most first versions: a small one-off build with standard tools — days of a developer's time, not a new project — and little to no ongoing fee at normal volumes. It's a line item in what apps cost in Australia, not a budget decision on its own.

Sending itself is free — Apple and Google don't charge per notification. You pay for developer hours to wire it in, plus a screen to manage messages.

What you're paying for (and what you're not)

The delivery plumbing is free. Apple and Google each run the system that carries notifications to phones — Apple's Push Notification service and Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, the free service most Android apps use. The documentation spells out the model: send at no charge. You pay for the work around it: connecting your app to receive messages, and a place for your team to write and send them.

A simple alert system (small add-on)

  • One message to everyone with the app
  • A basic send screen — a day or two of work
  • Usually folded into the existing build

A campaign system (costs more)

  • Schedule messages, target groups, track opens
  • A real message centre with drafts and history
  • Genuine hours — quote it as its own feature

Where it lands on your budget

Bolted onto an app already being built, notifications are a minor line. The extras are where founders get caught: photos in messages, quiet-hours rules and per-person settings all add hours. The real ongoing cost sits next door — the developer account, the store membership every app needs to publish at all (US$99 a year for Apple, US$25 once for Google — how app store fees work covers both). And because notifications travel through your hosting, the rented server space your app runs on, they add a trickle to a bill that grows with your audience — what app hosting costs shows the bands.

The pattern I see in quotes: founders budget for the feature, forget the management screen, then wonder why "send to everyone" is the only button. Ask which of the two you're buying — it usually explains the whole quote.
