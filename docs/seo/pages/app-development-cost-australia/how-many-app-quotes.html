Three quotes is the sweet spot. That's enough to see the real price range and few enough to compare properly. If you're asking how many quotes should I get for an app, give each developer the same brief, then ask each one to spell out what's excluded. That's where quotes hide their differences.

1quote: no way to judge
3quotes: the sweet spot
6+quotes: more noise than insight

These numbers are a rule of thumb, not a law. The aim is to learn what a fair price looks like for your app. Our guide to app development costs gives typical bands to check your quotes against.

How to run a fair quote round

  • Write one brief. One page with the problem, users, key features and budget range.
  • Send the same brief to everyone. Different information means different prices.
  • Mix your sources. For example, one local studio, one freelancer and one other option, so you see different price points.
  • Ask what's left out. Testing, store submission and admin tools are the usual gaps.
  • Ask for the same format. Costs split into planning, design, build and testing are far easier to compare.

Business.gov.au suggests learning about the other party's business before you negotiate. Take that seriously here. Look at the studio's past work and ask to speak to a past client. A price tells you what they charge, not how they work.

How many quotes should I get for a small or large app?

So how many quotes should I get for an app of my size? For a small app, two quotes can be enough if both are clear. For a large or high-risk build, four or five might be worth the time. Stop when new quotes stop teaching you anything. And don't automatically pick the lowest. A quote far below the others usually means something is missing, as we explain in our guide to reading an app development quote.

Be careful with any quote that arrives within an hour of you describing your idea. A fair estimate needs a proper look at scope first.
