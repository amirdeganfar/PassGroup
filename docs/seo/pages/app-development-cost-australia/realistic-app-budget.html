A budget that only covers the build is a budget for half a business. What is a realistic budget for an app startup? One that funds three things: a focused first build — usually the low-to-mid five figures in Australia — plus a first year of running costs, plus some money to market the thing. Founders who budget only the build run dry right when the app needs users. A realistic budget for an app startup = first build + first year of running costs + a marketing line. Leaving out the last two is the most common first-timer mistake we see.

What is a realistic budget for an app startup?

In dollars, for a typical Australian first release:

$30k–$60kthe build: a focused first app by a local team
$100–$500/morunning it: hosting, store fees, monitoring
10–20%of the build, held back for marketing and fixes

Bands move with scope. A lean app built by a freelancer can land under $30,000; a complex, multi-platform product pushes well past $100,000. The middle ground — where most startups live — is MVP development cost.

The three buckets to budget for

Bucket one, the build. One number, paid in stages. Scope decides it, so protect the scope. Before the number gets locked, it's worth a gut-check on whether the app is worth building — sometimes the honest answer saves you the whole budget.

Bucket two, running costs. Servers, store fees, updates. Even a quiet app costs $100–$500 a month to keep healthy — our guide to what it costs to run an app breaks the line items down. Call it $1,500–$6,000 for year one.

Bucket three, marketing. An app nobody finds earns nothing. You don't need a big figure — you need a real one. Moneysmart's free budget planner is a solid place to pressure-test whatever you set aside.

Whatever build number you land on, add 15–20% as a buffer. Every first build meets something nobody predicted — a store review delay, one rethink of onboarding, a device quirk.

Making the money stretch

Phase the work instead of shrinking it: build the one core flow first, put it in front of real users, then spend the next slice on what they proved they want. Sequencing the spend is also how the hidden costs of app development get dodged — surprises become decisions, not emergencies.

Is $10,000 a realistic budget for an app startup?

For a custom app by a professional team, no — it buys a prototype or a very small no-code build, not a shippable product. Treat $10,000 as research money, not launch money.

One last reframe helps: a realistic budget is the price of learning fast, not the price of perfection.
