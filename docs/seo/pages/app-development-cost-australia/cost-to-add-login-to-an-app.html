"Just a login" is the most common scope-shrink we hear, and it's rarely just a login. So how much does it cost to add logins to an app? For a first version built on a standard login service — a ready-made system that handles accounts for you — most budgets land between $2,000 and $8,000, with social sign-ins and staff roles pushing higher. It's one of the feature lines in the app development cost guide for Australia, and it deserves its own honesty.

What a login actually involves

An account looks like one box; it's a small system. People need to register, choose a password, forget that password, get a reset email, and see only what they're allowed to see. Every screen in the app must now check who's looking. In our guide to what affects app development cost, accounts are the factor clients underestimate most — a "quick login" growing into two weeks of work is a pattern we've watched more than once. Accounts also need somewhere to live: a server holds them, which is why most apps need one.

The good news: you're not building any of this from scratch. Developers plug in a standard login service and style it to match — that's what keeps the band at four figures instead of five. Logins matter most where security does, though — it's why banking apps spend serious money on this exact layer, and why we'd never shave the secure parts to hit a lower quote.

How much does it cost to add logins to an app, then?

Two to eight thousand dollars covers most first versions — email login at the floor, social sign-ins and staff roles at the ceiling. The extras are where quotes drift: "remember me" devices, profile editing, admin screens. None are wrong; they just need to be named in the quote, not discovered later.

Wherever else you save, don't save here. If your app holds anyone's personal details, Australian law expects you to protect them — the OAIC's notifiable data breaches scheme explains when a leak must be reported.

That's the trade in one line: logins are the cheapest thing in your budget to do well and the most expensive to do badly. When founders ask me where to trim, my answer is always the same — anywhere but this.
