Chat has a reputation problem: founders either hear "just messages" and expect it free, or hear "real-time messaging" and expect ruin. The truth sits in the middle. How much does it cost to add chat to an app? A few thousand dollars using a chat service — clearly more if it's built custom with typing indicators, media and moderation — plus a subscription that keeps running monthly. For the wider budget, the app development cost guide for Australia puts features like this in context.

Do: use a ready-made chat service

  • A chat service — messaging rented by the month — handles delivery, offline mode and sync
  • Your developer wires it into your screens: days, not weeks
  • The bill is modest at small scale and grows with usage

Don't: build chat from scratch first up

  • Custom chat means building message storage, sync and delivery yourself
  • You own the reliability work — messages can't be lost or doubled
  • Rarely worth it unless chat IS your product

What pushes the price up

Plain text chat is the cheap end; every extra below adds build time:

  • Typing indicators and read receipts — small touches, each takes time
  • Photo, video or file sharing — media storage and compression
  • Group chat — more logic than one-to-one
  • Moderation — reporting, blocking and filtering when strangers chat
  • Push notifications — the "new message" alert; Apple and Google provide this plumbing free

How much does it cost to add chat to an app like WhatsApp?

A WhatsApp-style experience — voice notes, calls, encryption — is a custom build with a price to match. That's a whole product, not a feature. Chat-heavy products such as dating apps show the custom end of the range; most business apps never need it.

The monthly tail after the build

Whatever you choose, chat keeps billing after launch: the service subscription continues monthly and scales with users and message volume. It behaves like every other running cost in an app's monthly bill — worth budgeting from day one, not discovering at invoice time.

I tell clients the same thing every time: nobody downloads your app for the chat. Use the service, and spend the savings on the part users actually see.

Short version: a few thousand dollars with a chat service, clearly more custom, and a monthly subscription that continues after the build. Pick the first option unless chat is the product.
