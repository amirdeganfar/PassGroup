The quote lands in your inbox — $45,000 — and the first question isn't the price. Can I pay for an app in instalments? Almost always, yes. Most developers work in staged payments: a deposit to book your slot, progress payments as phases finish, and a final payment at handover. Some studios offer monthly plans on top. What matters is how payments map to delivered work — never pay everything upfront. So, can I pay for an app in instalments?

Yes — but instalments tied to milestones, not to calendar months. A common shape:

  1. Deposit to start

    Books your place in the queue and covers discovery and design. Often 20–40% on a smaller build.

  2. Progress payments

    Paid as phases finish — designs signed off, build complete, testing passed. Each payment lands on software you can actually open and try.

  3. Final payment at handover

    Store submission, code handover, accounts transferred to you. Holding back a final slice keeps "finished" in everyone's interest.

The rhythm matters as much as the amounts. Each payment should answer one question: what can I see working right now?

Walk away from anyone demanding full payment upfront. Milestone payments are your leverage if a project stalls — it's why we never invoice a whole build in advance.

What to ask before you sign

Two questions sort fair payment terms from risky ones. First: what does each payment buy? The answer should name deliverables — signed-off designs, a testable build — not "month two". The app development cost breakdown shows what a normal phase includes.

Second: when do I get the code and accounts? Every payment should move you closer to owning the app outright, in your own developer accounts, not closer to a promise.

Monthly plans exist too. Some studios spread a fixed-price build across equal monthly amounts — effectively credit, so expect the total to be similar or a little higher. Either way, the payment basics from business.gov.au on payments and invoicing apply: agree the terms in writing before work starts.

How big should the deposit be?

Commonly 20–40% on small builds, less on larger staged projects. If a developer asks for 100% before starting, treat it as a red flag, not a discount opportunity.
