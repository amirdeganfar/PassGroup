Honest answer: sometimes. Is building an app worth it? It is when one focused job earns or saves more money than the build plus a year of running costs — and it's worth proving that cheaply before you build big. Excitement is easy to feel; the maths is what decides it. So before you look at prices, get your head around what an app actually costs in Australia, because every check below uses those numbers.

The three numbers to check first

Write these on one page. Can't fill them in yet? Then you're not ready to build — and finding that out now is the cheap part.

1What the build costs — one-off, and quotes vary a lot
2What it costs to run every month — hosting, updates, fixes
3What one job in the app earns or saves per month

Compare 1 and 2 against 3. If one job — taking bookings, showing stock, cutting admin hours — clearly pays for the build and the monthly bill within a year or two, the app is worth it. If the sums only work with 50,000 users on day one, that's a punt, not a plan. A realistic app budget shows what those figures look like for small Australian businesses, and running an app costs more each month than most founders expect — so use real numbers.

Usually worth it

  • The app does one job you'd otherwise pay staff to do
  • The maths works with a few hundred users
  • You'd still want it even if it never grew

Usually not worth it (yet)

  • The idea needs mass adoption before it earns a cent
  • "An app" is the goal, but the job isn't clear
  • The whole budget rides on version one being perfect

Prove it cheap before you build big

The biggest mistake I see founders make is spending the whole budget on version one. A smarter path is an MVP — a minimum viable product, meaning the smallest app that tests the idea. An MVP costs a fraction of a full build, and it turns "will people use this?" into a real answer. ASIC's free budgeting tools on Moneysmart are a plain way to write the money maths down first.

What I watch for in first meetings

The tell: founders who can't name the one job the app does, but can list ten features. The projects that go well start with one job, do it well, and grow from real usage — not from a long wish list.

So, is building an app worth it?

Yes — when one focused job earns or saves more than the build plus a year of running costs, and you've proven it cheaply first. If the numbers need thousands of users on day one, shrink the scope or walk away.
