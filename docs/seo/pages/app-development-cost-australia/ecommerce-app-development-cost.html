The short answer: ecommerce app development cost

The whole landscape fits on three cards:

$50k–$120kcustom single-brand shop app, typical AU build
$120k–$350k+marketplace-grade platform (many sellers)
~US$39/moentry plan on major hosted store platforms

Reading them left to right. A custom app is software built for your shop alone — your checkout flow, your loyalty rules, your integrations. A marketplace-grade platform is what you build when many sellers trade through your app and you handle onboarding, commissions and payouts. An off-the-shelf store (Shopify and its peers — services that give you a ready-made online shop) covers the majority of Australian retail needs for a monthly fee and a modest setup budget. Most "we need an app" conversations end happily in that third column — and the faster you know which column you're in, the less the whole exercise costs.

Timelines follow the same shape. A hosted store can be trading in weeks; a custom single-brand app typically runs two to five months; marketplace-grade work is a six-to-twelve-month project. Anyone promising a serious custom app faster is skipping the testing that keeps card data safe and carts working on every phone — and that skipping shows up later as abandoned checkouts and one-star reviews.

What adds cost: payments, inventory, accounts

Three systems turn a simple app into a serious one. Payments — taking cards is a solved problem (a typical build spends around $8,000 wiring in a gateway like Stripe, a widely used payment service); taking them well means wallets, saved cards, and refund flows, each with extra hours — our guide to adding in-app payments breaks that line item down to the dollar. Inventory — an app that sells a product you've already sold is worse than no app, so stock syncing between app, website and shop floor is essential, and every channel you add makes it harder. Accounts — logins, order history, saved addresses: each is modest alone, but together they're why shop apps sit in our mid-range band rather than the simple one.

Add security underneath all three — customer details and card data attract attention — and a small slice of project management to keep the moving parts honest. That's the anatomy of the price.

It's also the reason two quotes for "the same shop app" can sit $80,000 apart. One might include the refund and returns flows (every retailer needs them), buy-now-pay-later options (paying in instalments, now common in Australia) and a returns portal; the other might quote a straight catalogue-to-checkout build and call the rest "phase two". Neither is wrong — but until those features are written down, the numbers can't be compared honestly.

An app or a good mobile website for selling?

Here's the decision most retailers skip. More than three-quarters of ecommerce traffic is already mobile, and a fast mobile website requires no install, ranks in Google, and updates instantly. An app buys you a home-screen icon, push notifications and a smoother experience for your best repeat customers — real advantages, but only valuable once people already buy from you regularly. The Australian government's ecommerce guide for business treats selling online as one connected picture (site, payments, reviews, obligations), and we'd read it the same way: get the mobile website excellent first, then let it justify the app with data.

The app isn't the moat. The customers are — and some of the best app budgets start as mobile-web budgets that earned their upgrade.

Custom app vs off-the-shelf: what each really costs

Custom app ($50k–$120k+)

  • Every screen and rule built for your brand
  • Deep integrations: POS, warehouse, loyalty
  • You own the code outright
  • Months of build, then maintenance

Hosted platform (plan + setup)

  • Launch in weeks on proven rails
  • Apps and add-ons for extra features
  • Monthly plan + transaction fees forever
  • Design and setup typically $5,000–$20,000

Neither column is "the cheap one" in absolute terms — they're different bets. The hosted platform trades flexibility for speed and a recurring fee; the custom app trades a bigger upfront cost for ownership and exact fit. Shops doing unusual things (custom products, subscriptions with rules, B2B pricing by customer) outgrow the hosted rails; everyone else usually doesn't need to leave them.

Ongoing costs: hosting, gateways, updates

Whatever you build, the bill keeps a pulse. Hosted platforms bundle their hosting into the plan but charge transaction fees on top of your gateway's. A custom app carries its own hosting (typically tens to a few hundred dollars a month at small scale), gateway fees per order, and a maintenance rhythm — phone makers release new software every year, and your app must keep up. Plan on 10–20% of the build per year, and see our honest guide to stopping maintenance you don't need before you sign anyone's retainer. When a shop actually needs an app

Ticking most of these boxes? Then a custom app has earned a conversation:

  • Customers buy from you repeatedly (subscriptions, consumables, loyalty) — not once a year
  • Your mobile website converts well and you still see regulars wanting faster checkout
  • Push notifications have a real job — order updates, back-in-stock alerts, offers people asked for
  • You need features hosted platforms can't do: customer-specific pricing, unusual products, deep warehouse integration
  • The numbers work: the app's extra sales can carry a $50k–$120k build plus upkeep

If you ticked one or two, a better mobile website is usually the higher-return spend. That's a more useful answer than any quote — and it's the answer we give when it's true.
