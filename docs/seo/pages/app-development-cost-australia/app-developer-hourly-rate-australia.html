App developer hourly rate Australia: the short answer

Here are the bands we and other Australian studios work within. Treat them as planning figures — a specific project can sit outside them for good reasons the rest of this page explains.

Who you're payingTypical hourly band (AUD)What that buys
Offshore freelancer$25–$50Skilled work, with management and timezone overhead on you
Australian freelancer$80–$150Direct access to the person building; limited spare hands
Agency team (blended rate)$120–$200Designer, developers and testing under one project manager

Two patterns hide in that table. The first: the rate matters less than the hours, because two quotes built on different rates can land on the same total. The second: offshore rates look cheaper per hour but often assume more total hours once rework and management time are counted. Neither pattern makes one option right — it makes the comparison more honest.

Junior, mid and senior: what each tier charges

Within those bands, seniority does most of the sorting:

$60–$100junior — supervised work, simpler screens and fixes
$100–$150mid — owns features end to end, asks the right questions
$150–$200+senior — architecture, security and the jobs that rescue builds

The trap is judging the tier by the number alone. A senior at $150 an hour often costs less than a junior at $90, because experience doesn't just work faster — it removes hours. The pattern we see in rescue jobs: half the budget went to cheap hours solving problems a senior would have designed away in week one. Buy the tier the problem needs, not the tier the budget fancied.

Freelance vs agency vs in-house: who charges what

The same hourly work arrives in three wrappers, and each wrapper adds or removes cost:

Freelancer

  • $80–$150 an hour, direct to the person building
  • Leanest total cost for a focused build
  • One person's availability is the project's availability

Agency

  • $120–$200 blended across a team
  • Cover when someone is sick or someone leaves
  • Design, build and testing managed for you

In-house is the third wrapper, and it's priced differently again. Employed programmers in Australia earn a median of $2,537 a week — about $132,000 a year before super — on Jobs and Skills Australia's latest occupational data. A studio's hourly rate is higher than that wage by design: it has to cover super, leave, insurance, tools and the hours that aren't billable to anyone. A common rule of thumb is that a professional services bill rate runs 1.5–3 times the wage it rides on. Hiring in-house only wins when you have enough steady work to keep someone busy — what freelance app developers cost and what agencies charge cover that trade in detail.

What a rate should include (it's not just the hours)

When you compare two rates, you're really comparing what's hiding behind them. A rate that looks high might be carrying things a low rate silently drops:

  • Project management — someone planning, checking and translating, or you doing it
  • Testing — a proper pre-launch pass, not "the developer clicked around"
  • Non-billable time — meetings, estimates, rework when a phone OS changes
  • Insurance and licences — professional indemnity, developer accounts, tooling

The offshore rate card makes this sharpest. Offshore freelancers quote $25–$50 an hour and some are excellent, but every timezone-delayed question and every misunderstanding a shared office would have caught adds hours you pay for somewhere. The honest comparison is total cost, not rate.

How hourly rates turn into project quotes

The maths is simple: estimated hours × hourly rate = the core cost. Each feature is estimated in hours — a login system might take 30, an in-app chat well over 100 — and the hours become dollars. A mid-range app typically needs 600–1,200 hours, which is why agency quotes start where they do: 600 hours at a $120 blended rate is $72,000 before a line is written down.

Contracts come in two shapes here, and the difference matters. As business.gov.au explains on contractor contracts, agreements for labour or skills pay an hourly or daily rate, while agreements for a result usually pay a fixed fee. Hourly suits open-ended work where scope will move. Fixed suits a defined build, because the builder carries the estimate risk — which is why studios that quote fixed-price first work hard at scoping before they sign.

Reading a quote priced by the hour

One division makes any hourly quote readable: divide the total by the rate and count the hours. A $60,000 quote at $150 an hour is 400 hours — roughly five months of one developer's full-time time. If your app doesn't feel like five months of work, one of the assumptions is wrong, and it's cheaper to find out which before you sign than after.

Then ask what the hours cover. A quote worth accepting shows its line items — screens designed, features built, testing, a project-management slice — and states what's excluded. Ask two questions of every hourly line: what happens when the estimate is beaten, and who bills for the meeting where you find out. Good builders answer both without blinking; the rest you learn to walk away from.

The rate-card question we hear most

"Another studio charges $35 an hour — why are you higher?" Our answer is always the same working: divide both quotes by their rates and compare the hours. The cheap quote almost always assumes more hours, and more hours of the wrong kind — rework, waiting, explaining. Sometimes offshore genuinely wins on the totals, and when it does we say so. The rate card never decides a project; the hours do.

Two questions come up every time the rate card lands on the table:

What is a fair app developer hourly rate in Australia?

One that's transparent about hours and inclusions. As bands: $80–$150 for freelancers, $120–$200 blended for agencies. A rate at either extreme deserves one question — what's excluded to make it that cheap, or what's included to make it that dear.

The same logic answers the follow-up question founders ask a week later:

Do agencies charge more per hour than freelancers?

Usually, yes — typically $120–$200 blended versus $80–$150. The gap buys duplication: when one person is unavailable, the project doesn't stop, and design, testing and management travel with the rate.

Whatever the app developer hourly rate Australia conversation lands on for your build, the rate is the one number you should refuse to read alone. Hours, inclusions and ownership tell you what you're actually buying.
