The build is paid, version one is live, and the tax question lands: what does all this do at the ATO? The app development costs ATO lets you claim straight away are usually the running ones — hosting, subscriptions, updates. The build itself is often different: it can be treated as an asset and claimed over several years. Which bucket a dollar falls into changes your cash flow, so it's worth getting right early. One question decides most of it: does the spend keep the business running, or does it create something that lasts? Running costs are claimed in year one. The app — that's the lasting thing.

Expense vs asset: the core ATO question

Every dollar you pay a developer or a platform lands in one of two buckets. An expense is consumed quickly: this month's hosting bill, this year's design subscription. An asset lasts past the year and keeps earning its keep — and a custom app is squarely in that camp for most businesses. The tax law calls developed software "in-house software", and the ATO's in-house software rules set out the claim paths in detail. Same build, same invoice — the treatment depends on what the money created and when it was ready to use. A quick illustration: the $180-a-month analytics tool is an expense; the $60,000 booking app it feeds numbers into is an asset, even though both were paid from the same bank account in the same year.

Why some build costs get claimed straight away

Three common spends are deductible in the year you pay them. Periodic payments — anything billed month to month, like your backend services, analytics and design tools — are consumed as you go. Off-the-shelf software with an effective life of a year or less goes the same way. And smaller capital items: if you're a small business using the simplified depreciation rules, each asset costing under the current instant asset write-off limit can be deducted in full in the year it's first used or installed ready for use — and the write-off covers second-hand assets as well as new ones. The ATO's instant asset write-off page lists the current limits and who qualifies.

Why others are spread over years

The app development costs ATO makes you spread over time are the ones that create a lasting asset: a custom build over the threshold, or development still under way. A finished app that cost more than the instant write-off limit goes into a small business pool and is claimed as it depreciates. While the app is still in development, costs can sit in a software development pool, claimed at set rates as the project matures — nothing in the first year, then 30%, 30%, 30% and 10% across the following years, for expenditure from 1 July 2015 onwards. If you'd rather skip the pools, the software can simply be depreciated over its effective life — five years for software you started holding from 1 July 2015.

GST on app development invoices

GST is a separate question from the deduction one. You must register for goods and services tax (GST) once your turnover reaches $75,000 — business.gov.au's GST registration guide covers the thresholds. Once you're registered, the GST your developer charges on their invoices can generally be claimed back as a credit, so the deduction maths runs off the ex-GST amount. On the sales side, your prices then include GST — one-eleventh of the total, which you pass on to the ATO when it's due. Not registered yet? No credits, so budget the full invoice value either way. Two practical notes: check every developer invoice actually shows an ABN and the GST line, and remember registration is backdated to when you were meant to register — late registration doesn't erase the liability.

Records to keep from the first deposit

Whichever bucket your costs land in, the claim is only as good as the paperwork behind it. Start with a clear app development contract, because it dates the work and defines the milestones. Then keep:

  • Signed quotes and the contract, before work starts
  • Every tax invoice, showing the developer's ABN and the GST
  • Bank and card records that match each invoice
  • Milestone approvals — what you signed off, and when
  • A simple project diary: who built what, and when it went live

Why the diary? Because the year you claim the build often turns on when the app was first "installed ready for use". In our studio, that one-line record has settled more tax-time arguments than any clever structure — twelve months later, nobody else remembers the go-live date. Why this is a tax-agent question (and when to ask)

The buckets above are the map; your registered tax agent reads the terrain. Your structure (sole trader or company), the timing of "ready for use", GST registration, and — if the build involved genuine experimentation — whether the R&D tax incentive stacks on top, all shift the final answer. Ask before 30 June, not after: pool elections and timing decisions are locked in for the year, not reverse-engineered later. And if a developer invoice looks wrong, query it before the BAS, not after.
